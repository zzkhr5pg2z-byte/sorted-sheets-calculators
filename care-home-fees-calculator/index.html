How it works

While someone has more than the upper limit in savings, they pay the whole care home fee themselves. Their income (pensions and most benefits) goes towards it, and the rest comes out of savings. That gap is the shortfall.

The calculator goes month by month for up to 10 years. Each month it takes the shortfall out of their savings, and the fee goes up once a year in the month you pick. When savings fall to the upper limit, the council should start to help. It gives the date, and a date about 3 months before it to ask for a financial assessment, because assessments take time.

Between the two limits the council pays part of the fee. It counts £1 a week of "tariff income" for every £250 (or part of £250) of savings above the lower limit, and the calculator takes that from savings each month. Below the lower limit, savings are ignored.

Some of the fee can be paid for them. In England, someone in a nursing home gets NHS-funded nursing care of £267.68 a week, paid to the home. In Scotland, free personal care is £260.30 a week, plus £117.10 a week for nursing care in a nursing home, even if they pay for themselves. The calculator takes these off the fee. In Wales and Northern Ireland, ask the home or the council what applies.

Check this first: NHS Continuing Healthcare

If their main need is a health need, the NHS may pay the whole fee, whatever their savings. Ask the hospital, GP or council for an NHS Continuing Healthcare assessment before you plan around paying.

What it leaves out

  • The council's own assessment. It decides what counts and what they pay, and every council works its figures out itself.
  • Interest on savings, and any change in their income.
  • Benefits that change: Attendance Allowance usually stops once the council pays towards the fee.
  • Top-ups. Once the council helps, it pays its usual rate. If the home costs more, family may be asked to pay the difference.
  • Deferred payment agreements, which let the council pay now and be repaid from the home later.
  • Giving money or property away to get under the limits. The council can treat it as if they still had it ("deprivation of assets").

This is a planning tool, not financial or legal advice. For decisions about selling a home or paying for care, speak to the council, an independent financial adviser who specialises in care fees, or Age UK.
