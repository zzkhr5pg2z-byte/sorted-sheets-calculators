How it works

1. Your profit

Profit is your business income less your allowable expenses. Or you can take the £1,000 trading allowance off your income instead, which helps if your expenses are small. The calculator picks whichever gives the lower profit, unless you choose. The allowance can't be more than your income, so it can't make a loss.

2. Income Tax

Your profit and any job or pension pay are added together. The Personal Allowance of £12,570 is tax-free. It goes down by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000, so it's gone at £125,140. The rest is taxed in bands.

3. National Insurance

Class 4 is 6% of your profit between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% of profit above that. It's on your business profit only, not your job pay. With a profit of £6,845 or more, Class 2 is treated as paid, so there's nothing to pay and the year still counts for your State Pension. Below that you can choose to pay voluntary Class 2 at £3.50 a week.

4. What you pay in January

Your bill for 2025/26 is the Income Tax and Class 4, less the tax already taken off your job or pension pay, plus any voluntary Class 2. Take off any payments on account you made for 2025/26 last year, and what's left is the balancing payment.

HMRC then usually asks for 2 payments on account towards 2026/27, each half of this year's Income Tax and Class 4 (less the tax taken off your pay). The first is due with the balancing payment by 31 January 2027, the second by 31 July 2027. You don't need them if that tax is under £1,000, or if more than 80% of your tax was taken off your pay. That's why the first January bill often comes as a shock: it's this year's tax plus half as much again. If you know next year's profit will be lower, you can ask HMRC to reduce your payments on account.

Not included

  • Savings interest, dividends, rental income and capital gains. Student Loan repayments and the High Income Child Benefit Charge.
  • Pension contributions, Gift Aid and Marriage Allowance, which can lower your tax. Benefits from a job (P11D).
  • Capital allowances, and using a loss against other income.
  • If you're employed too, the National Insurance annual maximum could lower your Class 4. HMRC works that out.
  • More than one business, or a partnership.
