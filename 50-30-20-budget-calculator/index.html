How do I split my pay with the 50/30/20 rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to plan your money: half your take-home pay for the things you have to pay, 30% for the things you choose, and 20% for savings or paying off debt. Type your pay to see your split in pounds.

How it works

Your take-home pay is split three ways. If you type a year's pay, it's divided by 12 first, so every figure is for a month.

  • Needs, 50%: what you have to pay to live and work. Rent or mortgage, energy, water, council tax, the food shop, your phone, getting to work, and the minimum payment on any debts.
  • Wants, 30%: what you choose to spend on. Eating out, takeaways, streaming and other subscriptions, the gym, hobbies, clothes and treats.
  • Savings and debt, 20%: money for your future. An emergency fund, savings pots, investing, and paying debts off faster than the minimum.

Type what you really spend in each part to check it. Needs and wants are on track when they're at or under their share. Savings and debt is on track when it's at or over its share. Anything you leave empty counts as nothing, and what's left over is the pay you haven't put in any part yet.

If it doesn't fit

If your rent or mortgage is high, needs can come to more than half your pay on their own. The 50/30/20 split is a starting point, not a test. Take a little from wants first, and keep something going into savings, even if it's less than 20% for now.

  • It uses one month's pay. If yours changes from month to month, use a typical month, or a low one to be safe.
  • It doesn't know your bills or your debts: it only works out the split and compares what you type.
  • Payments you make once a year, such as car insurance or a TV licence, are easier to plan for if you divide them by 12 and count them each month.
