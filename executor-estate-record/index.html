Sorting out a parent's or relative's affairs after they've died? This spreadsheet keeps it all in one place, one step at a time.

List what they owned and owed, and it works out the estate's value and a simple inheritance tax estimate using the 2026/27 rules: the £325,000 nil-rate band, the £175,000 residence band for a home left to children or grandchildren (and its taper over £2 million), unused bands from a late husband, wife or civil partner, and gifts in the 7 years before death. It lists who to tell, works out the key time limits from the date of death for England, Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland, keeps an estate account with a running balance, and shows what each beneficiary is still due.

It's a record-keeping tool and an estimate, not legal or tax advice, and it says when to get professional help.

What it does

  • Assets and debts with their share (for joint accounts) and whether each counts for inheritance tax
  • Inheritance tax estimate, step by step: nil-rate band, residence nil-rate band and taper, transferred bands from a late spouse or civil partner, gifts in the last 7 years, spouse and charity exemptions
  • Who to tell: Tell Us Once in England, Scotland and Wales, the Bereavement Service in Northern Ireland, banks, pensions, insurers, utilities and more, with date told, reference and done
  • Steps and time limits for their nation: registering the death, paying inheritance tax (end of the 6th month), IHT400 (12 months), creditors' notices, deed of variation (2 years)
  • Probate (England, Wales, Northern Ireland) or confirmation (Scotland), named for you
  • Estate account with a running balance, and a summary of what's held now
  • Beneficiaries: gifts in the will paid first, then shares of what's left, with money kept back for final costs
