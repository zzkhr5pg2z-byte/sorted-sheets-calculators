How the 7-year rule works

Most gifts to people are "potentially exempt". If the person who gave them lives for 7 more years, there's no inheritance tax on them. If they die within 7 years, the gifts are added up in date order and use the £325,000 nil-rate band first. Tax at 40% is only due on gifts above it, and less for gifts made 3 to 7 years before the death:

Gifts that are exempt straight away

  • Annual exemption: £3,000 of gifts each tax year (6 April to 5 April). Any part not used can be carried forward to the next tax year only. The calculator uses it on the earliest gifts in each tax year that no other exemption covers, and uses this year's allowance before last year's leftover.
  • Small gifts: gifts of up to £250 per person per tax year, as long as you haven't used another allowance on the same person. If gifts to one person add up to more than that in a tax year, the calculator doesn't count any of them as small gifts (it matches people by the name you type).
  • Wedding or civil partnership gifts: up to £5,000 to a child, £2,500 to a grandchild or great-grandchild, £1,000 to anyone else. Anything over the limit is treated like any other gift.
  • Husband, wife or civil partner, and charities: exempt with no limit.
  • Regular gifts from surplus income: exempt with no limit, but only if they're regular, paid from income rather than savings, and leave enough to live on as normal. Executors have to show this to HMRC, so keep records each year.

What it leaves out

  • The rest of the estate, the residence nil-rate band, and any nil-rate band passed on by a husband, wife or civil partner. This is about the gifts.
  • Gifts into most trusts, which are taxed differently and can be taxed straight away.
  • Gifts with strings attached, like giving away a house but still living in it rent-free. These can still count as part of the estate.
  • Business Relief and Agricultural Relief.

This is a record-keeping and planning tool, not tax or legal advice. For large gifts, trusts or business assets, speak to a solicitor or tax adviser.
