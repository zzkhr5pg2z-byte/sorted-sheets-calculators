No Gift Aid. A charity can only claim it when the donor has made a declaration and paid enough tax. If they're not sure, it's safer not to tick the Gift Aid box: if HMRC pays back more than they paid in tax, they may be asked to pay the difference.

How it works

Donations from UK taxpayers are treated as if basic rate tax has already been taken off them. The charity claims that tax back from HMRC: 25p for every £1 given. So £100 becomes £125 for the charity, and it costs the donor nothing extra.

The donor must make a Gift Aid declaration for each charity, and must have paid enough tax. Their donations in a tax year can't be more than 4 times the Income Tax or Capital Gains Tax they paid that year. That's why the calculator shows the tax needed: a quarter of the donation.

Higher and additional rate taxpayers

If the donor pays tax above the basic rate, they can claim back the difference between the tax they paid on the donation and what the charity got, through their Self Assessment tax return or by asking HMRC to change their tax code. This calculator doesn't work that part out. gov.uk's example: a 40% taxpayer gives £100, the charity claims Gift Aid to make it £125, and the donor can claim back £25.

When a charity can't claim it

  • Payments for goods or services, or donations where the donor gets a benefit over a certain limit.
  • Donations from limited companies, or made through Payroll Giving.
  • Donations of shares, or by charity cards or vouchers.
  • There are special rules for sponsored challenges, membership fees, church collections, charity shops, events and auctions.

For charities: the Gift Aid Small Donations Scheme (a top-up on small cash and contactless gifts) isn't included here. This calculator is for information, not tax advice.
